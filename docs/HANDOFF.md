## Honest DRAFT reading
- **RL vs straight-line: RL wins** on success (80.4% vs 65.5%; it solves on average 34 of the 69 maps straight-line fails) and on time where both succeed (per-map time ratio 0.973 +/- 0.012, CI 0.955-0.989). It does not save significant energy (ratio 0.994, CI includes 1).
- **RL vs classical planner: RL loses** on every metric. Planner success 95% vs 80%; on maps both solve RL needs 10.4% more time (CI 9.2-15.5%) and 5.1% more energy (CI 4.0-9.0%) than the energy-aware planner. Diagnosis: the planner sees the whole current field (privileged global information) and solves a near-optimal shortest-path problem; RL sees only an 8 x 8 km patch, so it cannot plan around eddies it has not reached yet. 16 maps are solved by the planner but by no RL seed. This is the expected ordering for a local-sensing policy vs a full-knowledge planner and should be reported as such, not as "RL beats classical planning".
- **Energy model limits the energy story.** Energy = hotel load x time + turning, so energy mostly tracks time. The Pareto sweep shows no real time/energy trade-off: any positive energy weight just suppresses turning (RL energy 57.5 +/- 7.9 kJ at ratio 0 vs 45.2-46.3 kJ for ratios 0.25-16; differences among those within seed std). The planner dominates RL on both axes at every ratio.
- **Ablations (3 seeds)**: no current observation -> 66.8% (straight-line level): RL's advantage comes from using the flow. No energy term -> 78.5% success but energy 1.72x straight-line (constant turning). Single training map -> 50.3 +/- 15.0% on unseen maps (worse than straight-line); the fixed map (train seed 0) happens to be hard.
- **Robustness**: Gaussian noise on observed currents up to 0.3 m/s leaves RL success unchanged (80.6-81.0%) but raises its energy ratio to 1.64 (jittery steering). Baselines read true currents, so they are flat by construction (asymmetric test). Held-out double gyre: straight 96%, planner 99.5-100%, RL 98.8 +/- 0.6%; RL energy ratio 0.952 +/- 0.013 (planner 0.97) but slower than the planner (24.5 vs 23.1 h). This field is easier than the training mix.
- **Training budget / stability (two pre-registered extras)**: 6M steps made RL worse (75.0 +/- 3.2%) because PPO became unstable (policy std collapsed to 0.04, clip fraction 0.2-0.29, KL spikes 0.57). Adding linear learning-rate decay + target_kl 0.02 at 2M steps gave 85.0 +/- 2.6% success [80.4, 89.0], a significant 2.7% energy saving vs straight-line (ratio 0.973, CI 0.955-0.992), but still 3.3% more energy and 9.3% more time than the planner. This variant was designed after the main test results were known, so treat it as exploratory; a confirmatory test on a fresh map set (e.g. seeds 200000-200199) is the clean next step.

## Deviations from the spec (details in docs/ASSUMPTIONS.md)
- Observation in a goal-aligned frame plus world position and goal bearing (62 entries); world-frame version learned only ~5% success in the 300k check.
- torch from PyPI (CUDA build running on CPU) because the CPU wheel index is blocked here.
- PPO: 2M steps per run; 3 seeds (not 5) for each Pareto weight and ablation; main uses 5.
- Pareto weights normalised to w_time + w_energy = 2.
- Random policy excluded from the strict map pairing (protocol amendment, dated).

## Known issues
- TensorBoard event files of `main_s*` are incomplete (a git autostash replaced open files mid-training); `episodes.csv` / `progress.csv` were recovered in full from /proc and are what the figures use.
- Planner path follower turns ~5x more than straight-line (turning energy ~5-6 kJ vs ~0.9 kJ per episode); a smoother tracking controller would likely lower planner energy further.
- Baselines use the true current field (privileged); observation-noise tests therefore only perturb RL.
- 2D kinematic model, no dive cycle, no buoyancy-pump energy; energy model is an assumption.
- The `.venv` with the CUDA torch build is 5.7 GB (not committed).
